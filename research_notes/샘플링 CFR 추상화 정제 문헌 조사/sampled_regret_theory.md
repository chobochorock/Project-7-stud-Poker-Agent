## Q1. Lanctot, Waugh, Zinkevich, Bowling (NeurIPS 2009): exact high-probability bounds for outcome and external sampling, and the role of δ

### Takeaway
MCCFR's original guarantees hold with probability 1−p. They bound **average overall regret** (summed over infosets through M_i), not per-infoset regret. Their dependence on p is polynomial, (1+√(2/p)), because the proof only uses second moments. External sampling has δ=1. Outcome sampling pays 1/δ, where δ lower-bounds the probability of sampling any terminal history, and δ can be exponentially small in depth. These bounds are too loose to calibrate statistical split tests. Use Azuma or Freedman per infoset instead (see Q3).

### Cited Findings
- [V] The paper defines the MCCFR family. In it, sampled counterfactual-regret updates equal CFR's updates in expectation, and both outcome sampling (OS) and external sampling (ES) "have bounded overall regret with high probability." — [NeurIPS 2009 abstract](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2009/hash/00411460f7c92d2124a67ea0f4cb5f85-Abstract.html)
- [V] There was "an error in the original paper appearing in the NIPS proceedings," and a corrected version exists. Cite the corrected PDF and errata, not the proceedings version. — [Bowling publication page](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~bowling/publications/b2hd-09nips-mccfr-w-trws.html)
- [V] **ES bound** (Theorem 20 in the supplementary appendix): "For any p ∈ (0,1], when using external-sampling MCCFR, with probability at least 1−p, average overall regret is bounded by **R_i^T ≤ (1 + √2/√p) · Δ_{u,i} · M_i · √|A_i| / √T**." — [Supplementary appendix](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr_appendix.pdf); [paper (author copy)](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf)
- [V2] **OS bound**: the same form with an extra factor 1/δ, i.e. (1+√(2/p))·(1/δ)·Δ·M_i·√|A_i|/√T. One citing paper notes that "δ = 1 in external-sampling MCCFR." Another restates it with 1/δ and attributes it to the OS theorem (Theorem 5). The search tool flagged this difference in attribution between secondary sources. The primary text gives OS the 1/δ form and ES the δ=1 form. — [Tang et al. 2024, arXiv 2411.00954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00954); [Schmid, "Search in Imperfect Information Games", arXiv 2111.05884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05884)
- [R] δ in OS: the theorem requires, at every timestep and for every z ∈ Z, either π_{−i}(z)=0 or q(z) ≥ δ > 0, where q(z) is the probability of sampling terminal history z. Under ε-on-policy exploration at the traverser's nodes, q(z) is a product of ≥ε/|A| factors, so 1/δ can grow exponentially with the number of the traverser's decisions on a path. — [paper](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf)
- [R] General MCCFR theorem in the corrected version: if a sum over sampling blocks of squared importance-weighted reach ratios (π_i(z[I],z)·π_{−i}(z[I])/q(z)) is at most 1/δ² for every block of infosets, then the same (1+√2/√p)(1/δ)Δ M_i √|A_i|/√T bound holds. I did not reproduce the exact indexing of the condition. — [paper](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf); [appendix](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/%7Ebowling/papers/09nips-mccfr-appendix.pdf)
- [R] M_i = Σ_{B∈𝔅_i} √|B|. Here 𝔅_i partitions player i's infosets so that infosets in one part share player i's own action sequence. This gives √|𝓘_i| ≤ M_i ≤ |𝓘_i|, and the same paper introduced the "tighter" CFR bound Δ M_i √|A_i|/√T. — [paper](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf)
- [V] The authors use 95% confidence intervals only to report experimental curves. They do not use them to drive the algorithm. — [paper](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr.pdf) (via search excerpt)
- [V] Farina, Kroer and Sandholm (2020) later improved MCCFR's dependence on p from O(√(1/p)) to O(√log(1/p)). — [arXiv 2002.08493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08493)

### Inferences
- [D] At p=10⁻⁶ (a Bonferroni-style budget over many buckets or epochs), (1+√(2/p)) ≈ 1415, while √(2 ln(1/p)) ≈ 5.3. Any union bound over many buckets × epochs needs Azuma, Freedman or Bernstein-type tails. Chebyshev-type tails like Lanctot's are too weak.
- [D] The 2009 bounds cover *overall* average regret under **perfect recall**. They do not directly bound the regret of an *original* infoset while it is pooled in an imperfect-recall bucket. That needs the well-formed or skew-well-formed analysis (Q6) or explicit per-original-infoset tracking, which is what the requester does.
- [D] For a *single* infoset under ES with perfect recall, at most one history of I is reached per traversal. Opponent and chance reach is then cancelled exactly by the sampling probability, so |r̃_t(I,a)| ≤ 2·max|u|. These bounded martingale increments make per-infoset Azuma or Freedman bounds straightforward. Under OS, increments scale with 1/q(z) and are heavy-tailed.
- [D] Under ES, r̃_t(I,·)=0 on every iteration where I is not reached. The per-iteration variance is therefore about π_{−i}(I)·Δ² and the mean is about π_{−i}(I)·(regret rate). Signal-to-noise per iteration scales like √π_{−i}(I). Rarely reached original infosets need about 1/π_{−i}(I) more iterations to reach a split decision at the same confidence. Variance-adaptive tails (Freedman or empirical Bernstein) matter much more than Hoeffding here.

### Gaps
- I could not read the corrected theorem text, so the exact indexing of the general "1/δ²" condition and the exact OS theorem number are unverified.
- I did not check Lanctot's PhD thesis (2013), which reportedly restates and refines these bounds, because full-text access was blocked.

## Q2. Gibson et al., "Generalized Sampling and Variance in CFR" (AAAI 2012) and "Efficient MCCFR in Games with Many Player Actions" (NeurIPS 2012)

### Takeaway
AAAI 2012 shows that any **bounded, unbiased** estimator of counterfactual values gives probabilistic regret minimization. Its regret bound is stated in terms of the estimator's **variance**, so lower variance means fewer iterations. It also introduces "probing" for non-sampled actions. NeurIPS 2012 introduces Average Strategy Sampling (AS), which samples only a subset of the traverser's own actions. For split testing, this paper is the main theoretical support for treating estimator variance as the quantity to control. It does not supply per-infoset confidence bounds.

### Cited Findings
- [V] The AAAI 2012 paper generalizes MCCFR so that "any choice of an estimator can be used to probabilistically minimize regret, provided the estimator is bounded and unbiased." — [AAAI 2012 PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8241/8241-13-11769-1-2-20201228.pdf); [UAlberta copy](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/AAAI12-generalmccfr.pdf); [tech report](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/generalized-mccfr-tr.pdf)
- [V] It proves "a new upper bound on the average regret in terms of the variance of the estimates." An estimator with lower variance yields a smaller bound after T iterations, and so needs fewer iterations for a fixed ε. — [AAAI 2012](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8241/8241-13-11769-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- [V] It introduces a CFR sampling algorithm outside the MCCFR family that "probes" the values of non-sampled actions. — [AAAI 2012](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/AAAI12-generalmccfr.pdf)
- [R] The bound keeps the 1/√p (Chebyshev-type) dependence of Lanctot 2009. Roughly, it is (Δ-like term + √(variance term)/√p)·M_i·√|A_i|/√T, so the 1/δ importance-weight range is replaced by a variance term. The exact form and constants are unverified. — [AAAI 2012](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8241/8241-13-11769-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- [V] NeurIPS 2012 (Gibson, Burch, Lanctot, Szafron) introduces **Average Strategy Sampling**. It samples a subset of the traverser's actions according to the player's average strategy, motivated by a tighter iteration bound for CFR, and shows "a similar tighter bound for AS and other MCCFR variants." It converges faster in no-limit poker and Bluff. — [NeurIPS 2012 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/3df1d4b96d8976ff5986393e8767f5b2-Paper.pdf); [author copy](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/12-nips-avg-sampling.pdf)
- [R] AS samples action a with probability max{ε, (β + τ·s(I,a)) / (β + Σ_b s(I,b))}, where s holds cumulative average-strategy weights and ε, τ, β are parameters. — [NeurIPS 2012](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/12-nips-avg-sampling.pdf)

### Inferences
- [D] AS, OS and probing all add **importance weights on the traverser's own actions**. Per-infoset sampled regrets then lose ES's bounded-increment property. A split test calibrated for ES would be invalid if AS-style action subsampling were switched on later.
- [D] The bounded-and-unbiased condition is what keeps the requester's scheme theoretically sound. Any variance-reduction or baseline trick (Q4) that keeps conditional unbiasedness also keeps the AAAI 2012 and Farina 2020 guarantees.

### Gaps
- I could not see the exact AAAI 2012 theorem, the probing estimator's definition, or the NeurIPS 2012 "tighter bound." Gibson's PhD thesis (2014) was not checked.

## Q3. Farina, Kroer, Sandholm, "Stochastic Regret Minimization in EFGs" (ICML 2020): framework, bounds, and which minimizers work

### Takeaway
Any deterministic regret minimizer can be fed unbiased stochastic gradient (counterfactual-value) estimates. Its true regret then exceeds its regret on the sampled losses by an Azuma–Hoeffding term of order (M+M̃)√(T log(1/p)). Because the minimizer sees the sampled losses as an arbitrary adversarial sequence, RM, RM+, CFR, CFR+, FTRL and OMD are all covered **in theory**. RM+ with sampling is not a theorem-level failure. It is an empirical and constant-factor problem: M̃ is the range of the estimator, not of the true losses. This is the right template for per-infoset, per-window concentration in the requester's scheme.

### Cited Findings
- [V] The framework "allows us to use any regret-minimization algorithm, coupled with any gradient estimator," and the paper derives high-probability bounds for the combination. MCCFR's regret is shown to be only O(√log(1/p)) worse than CFR's, improving the earlier O(√(1/p)). — [arXiv 2002.08493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08493); [PMLR PDF](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/farina20a/farina20a.pdf)
- [V] The key result is **Proposition 1 (Sec. 3.1)**, proved by the Azuma–Hoeffding inequality. If R̃ (regret on the estimated losses) grows sublinearly in T, then R (true regret) also grows sublinearly with high probability. — [PMLR PDF](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/farina20a/farina20a.pdf)
- [R] Prop. 1's form: with probability ≥ 1−p, **R^T ≤ R̃^T + (M + M̃)·√(2T·log(1/p))**, where M and M̃ bound the true and estimated loss differences ⟨ℓ, x − x'⟩ over the decision set. The constants are unverified. — [MIT copy](https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2020/stochastic-rm-icml20/stochastic-rm.icml20.pdf)
- [V] Estimates must be conditionally unbiased, E_t[ℓ̃^t] = ℓ^t. For the saddle-point gap, the per-player inequalities are summed and a union bound gives probability ≥ 1−2p. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.08493)
- [V] The paper introduces a **balanced outcome-sampling** estimator "ensuring more stable gradient estimates." Experiments instantiate FTRL and OMD with the dilated-entropy DGF and compare them with MCCFR (ES and balanced OS). Each algorithm tries stepsizes {0.1, 1, 10, 100} and only the best is shown. FTRL and OMD beat MCCFR in several games, but in most of those games only the best stepsize does. — [arXiv 2002.08493](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08493); [review summary](https://liner.com/review/stochastic-regret-minimization-in-extensiveform-games)
- [V] Related bandit-feedback work gives sample-complexity guarantees for EFG learning. Bai, Jin, Mei, Yu (ICML 2022) propose Balanced OMD and Balanced CFR, which need Õ((XA+YB)/ε²) episodes, matching the information-theoretic lower bound up to logs. — [PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/bai22b.html). Other model-free high-probability work includes Farina & Sandholm (AAAI 2021) — [AAAI PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/16678/16678-13-20172-1-2-20210518.pdf) — and Kozuno et al. (NeurIPS 2021) — [PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/646c9941d7fb1bc793a7929328ae3f2f-Paper.pdf). I saw only their titles and abstracts in search results.

### Inferences
- [D] Prop. 1's proof needs (i) x^t to be F_{t−1}-measurable, i.e. chosen before sampling at t, which RM+ satisfies, and (ii) E[ℓ̃^t | F_{t−1}] = ℓ^t. The same argument applies **locally**. For each tracked original infoset I, action a and window W, Σ_{t∈W}(r̃_t(I,a) − r_t(I,a)) is a martingale with bounded increments under ES. This gives a valid per-infoset, per-window bound of order c·√(2|W| log(2/p)). Freedman's inequality, or the empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences in Q6, give variance-adaptive versions.
- [D] Prop. 1 assumes a **fixed decision set**. Splitting buckets changes the strategy space mid-run. If each split is decided from F_{t−1} information (it is predictable) and takes effect at a window boundary, the martingale argument still holds within each window. The overall guarantee then becomes a sum of per-window bounds (see Q5).
- [D] For RM+ under sampling, R̃^T ≤ M̃·√(|A|T). The regret bound scales with the *estimator's* range M̃. Under ES this is bounded, about 2Δ. Under OS it is about Δ/δ. This is a quantitative reason why RM+ (and RM) degrade under high-variance estimators, but it is not specific to RM+.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Prop. 1's exact constants, or whether the paper ran CFR+ or RM+ as one of the "regret minimizers" in experiments.

## Q4. Variance reduction: VR-MCCFR (Schmid et al., AAAI 2019), baseline-corrected estimators, Davis et al. (ICML 2020), and related work

### Takeaway
Control-variate baselines keep sampled counterfactual values **unbiased** and can cut variance by orders of magnitude. With an oracle or predictive baseline, variance can be exactly zero under some sampling schemes. VR-MCCFR explicitly reports that this variance reduction is what first made **CFR+ usable with sampling**. For split tests, baselines are the main way to shorten detection time. The condition is that the baseline is learned only from past iterations (predictable), so the martingale structure is preserved.

### Cited Findings
- [V] VR-MCCFR reformulates the sampled value estimate as a control variate, using state-action baselines and recursive bootstrapping. With a perfect baseline, value-estimate variance can be reduced to zero. — [arXiv 1809.03057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03057); [AAAI-19](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4048)
- [V] The abstract reports an order-of-magnitude speedup with empirical variance down three orders of magnitude, and continues: "the decreased variance allows for the first time CFR+ to be used with sampling, increasing the speedup to two orders of magnitude." These are the authors' own Leduc experiments. — [AAAI-19 PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4048/3926); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03057)
- [V2] A secondary summary paraphrases: before VR-MCCFR, "MCCFR+ performed no better than standard MCCFR due to the high variance interfering with CFR+'s aggressive regret-matching scheme." — [emergentmind topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/monte-carlo-counterfactual-regret-minimization-cfr)
- [V] Davis, Schmid, Bowling (ICML 2020) propose a **predictive baseline** that is provably optimal under certain sampling schemes. Their slides state that it gives zero-variance value estimates along sampled trajectories when public outcomes are sampled and every outcome is sampled at least once, which allows exact CFR updates on those trajectories. They also give learned history and infoset baselines. — [PMLR PDF](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/davis20a/davis20a.pdf); [ICML slides](https://ICML.cc/media/icml-2020/Slides/6002.pdf); [arXiv 1907.09633](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09633)
- [V] ESCHER (McAleer, Farina, Lanctot, Sandholm; ICLR 2023) explains the variance problem. Outcome-sampling-style estimators "divide the utility of each terminal state by a reach probability, which is often tiny," producing "estimates with extremely large magnitudes and high variance." DREAM inherits this. ESCHER is an unbiased, model-free method without importance sampling, using a learned history value function, and reports regret-estimate variance orders of magnitude lower. — [arXiv 2206.04122](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.04122); [DREAM arXiv 2006.10410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10410)
- [V] For **evaluation**, not solving, AIVAT reported an 85% reduction in standard deviation in a man-machine poker match, i.e. 44× fewer games for the same conclusion. — [AIVAT, AAAI 2018](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/11481)

### Inferences
- [D] A baseline b_t must be F_{t−1}-measurable (fit on past iterations only) for r̃ to remain a martingale-difference correction. Otherwise Q3's concentration and the requester's Borel–Cantelli argument break. AV-AIVAT (Q6) imposes the same "learn only from past games" rule for anytime-valid evaluation.
- [D] ES already removes importance weights for opponent and chance nodes. Its residual variance comes from *which* chance and opponent branch gets sampled, and in particular from whether I is reached at all. History or infoset baselines reduce the variance of r̃(I,a) − r̃(I,b) contrasts *given* I is reached. They do not fix the √π_{−i}(I) reach-rate problem from Q1. Stratifying or forcing visits to tracked infosets would, and must be compensated by importance weights.

### Gaps
- I did not verify any quantitative result for ES specifically. Most baseline results are for OS or public outcome sampling.

## Q5. RM+ / CFR+ with sampling: reported problems; tracking and window properties of RM+; effects of discounting

### Takeaway
(1) The literature consistently reports that CFR+'s advantage **does not carry over to MCCFR** without variance reduction or mini-batching. The sources are Burch's thesis (2017, as cited by others), VR-MCCFR (2019) and the Deep CFR and DCFR papers' preference for Linear CFR or DCFR under approximation error. RM+ is also unstable even without noise (Farina et al., NeurIPS 2023). (2) The inequality Q_{t+1} ≥ Q_t + r_t holds by definition. It makes RM+ **weakly adaptive**: regret on *every* window is ≤ ‖Q‖ ≤ Δ√(|A|T). Without a restart this is O(√T), not O(√|window|). I found no paper that states this property explicitly. (3) Discounting (DCFR or LCFR on Q) breaks the inequality. Only *discounted* window regret is then controlled.

### Cited Findings
- [V] VR-MCCFR: "allows for the first time CFR+ to be used with sampling." — [arXiv 1809.03057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03057)
- [V2] Citing papers attribute to Burch (2017): "CFR+ provides a massive improvement over CFR in the unsampled case, [but] the changes present in CFR+ (a floor on regret at zero and linear averaging) do not lead to superior performance when applied to MCCFR," and "Burch (2017) showed that MCCFR+ actually converge[s] slower than MCCFR when mini-batch is not used." [R] I believe the first sentence is in VR-MCCFR and the second in Li et al.'s "Double Neural CFR" (ICLR 2020), which used mini-batch MCCFR+. The search tool did not attribute them. — [Burch PhD thesis 2017](https://poker.cs.ualberta.ca/publications/Burch_Neil_E_201712_PhD.pdf); [VR-MCCFR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.03057)
- [V] Deep CFR (Brown, Lerer, Gross, Sandholm; ICML 2019): "most of these faster variants of CFR do not handle approximation error well," while Linear CFR tolerates approximation error well. — [PMLR v97](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19b/brown19b.pdf)
- [V] DCFR (Brown & Sandholm, AAAI 2019) multiplies accumulated **positive** regrets by t^α/(t^α+1) and **negative** regrets by t^β/(t^β+1), and weights average-strategy contributions by (t/(t+1))^γ, on each iteration. α=3/2, β=0, γ=2 was "consistently stronger than CFR+." CFR+ with t² averaging equals DCFR_{∞,−∞,2}. The paper's DCFR theorem gives a 6Δ|I|(√|A| + 1/√T)/√T-Nash average (the excerpt rendered the formula imperfectly), a constant factor worse than CFR. The paper has a "Discounted Monte Carlo CFR" section whose results I could not read. — [arXiv 1809.04040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.04040)
- [V2] Pluribus's blueprint MCCFR used linear-weighted discounting early in training and negative-regret pruning later. — [secondary blog](https://juejin.cn/post/7245163938484158525); [Science paper record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10119653). [R] The exact thresholds are unverified: actions with regret below −300M skipped on 95% of iterations, a regret floor around −310M, and discounting for the first 400 minutes.
- [V2, single 2026 preprint] Correlated-chance-sampling MCCFR is tested together with DCFR and LCFR update rules on Leduc and Goofspiel-4. It reports results with paired-bootstrap confidence intervals, which are used for reporting only. — [arXiv 2607.27035](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27035)
- [R] RM+ update: Q^{t+1}(a) = max(0, Q^t(a) + r^t(a)), Q^0 = 0, with σ^t ∝ Q^t. Tammelin et al. show Q^T(a) ≥ R^T(a) and ‖Q^T‖₂ ≤ Δ√(|A|T). — [Tammelin 2014, arXiv 1407.5042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.5042)
- [V2] "RM+ enjoys the same regret bounds as RM does." — [Schmid, arXiv 2111.05884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05884)
- [V] "Revisiting CFR+ and Alternating Updates" (Burch, Moravčík, Schmid; JAIR 2019): CFR+ "was introduced with a theoretical upper bound on solution error, but subsequent work showed an error in one step of the proof." The paper provides updated proofs that recover the original bound, including for alternating updates. — [JAIR PDF](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/11370/26477); [arXiv 1810.11542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.11542)
- [V] "Regret Matching+: (In)Stability and Fast Convergence in Games" (Farina, Grand-Clément, Kroer, Lee, Luo; NeurIPS 2023) gives counterexamples where RM+ and predictive RM+ "can be unstable, which might cause other players to suffer large regret." The fixes are "restarting and chopping off the positive orthant," which give O(T^{1/4}) individual and O(1) social regret in normal-form games. — [arXiv 2305.14709](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14709); [NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/c209cd57e13f3344a4cad4ce84d0ee1b-Abstract.html)
- [V] Adaptive and tracking regret background. *Adaptive regret* (Hazan & Seshadhri 2007) is the maximum regret over any contiguous interval. *Strongly adaptive* or interval regret (Daniely et al. 2015) is measured in terms of the interval length |I|, with best bounds of order O(√(|I| log T)). An algorithm with low adaptive regret "will automatically have low tracking regret … by summing the adaptive bound over all blocks of a segmentation," and "the converse is not always possible." — [Zhang et al., AISTATS 2020](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/zhang20a/zhang20a.pdf); [JMLR 2025 survey-style paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume26/23-1188/23-1188.pdf); [arXiv 2207.00646](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00646)
- [V] I found no source that analyzes RM+ as an adaptive or tracking regret minimizer. A targeted search returned only general adaptive-regret papers.

### Inferences
- [D] **The window property, precisely.** RM+ gives Q_{t+1} = [Q_t + r_t]^+ ≥ Q_t + r_t componentwise. Telescoping over any window W=[s,e]: Σ_{t∈W} r_t(a) ≤ Q_{e+1}(a) − Q_s(a) ≤ Q_{e+1}(a). Because σ_t ∝ Q_t and r_t = u_t − ⟨σ_t,u_t⟩𝟙, we have ⟨Q_t, r_t⟩ = 0, so ‖Q_{t+1}‖² ≤ ‖Q_t‖² + ‖r_t‖² and ‖Q_{e+1}‖ ≤ √(‖Q_s‖² + Σ_{t∈W}‖r_t‖²).
  - *Without* resetting Q at window starts, window regret ≤ Δ√(|A|·e). This is O(√T) for every window, which is weakly adaptive. A window of length L ≪ √T gets no useful guarantee: its average regret bound is about √T/L.
  - *With* a reset (Q_s = 0, a "restart"), window regret ≤ Δ√(|A|L). Over K windows, Σ_k Δ√(|A|L_k) ≤ Δ√(|A|KT) by Cauchy–Schwarz. Average regret → 0 iff K = o(T); geometric epochs give K = O(log T). This is the standard restart or doubling-trick argument. Restarting is also one of Farina et al.'s (2023) stabilizers.
  - Hence: the claim that Q_{t+1} ≥ Q_t + r_t gives window-regret control is **correct as an inequality**. The control is only O(√T) per window unless Q is reset.
- [D] **"No regret-transfer error at splits."** The telescoped bound Σ_W r ≤ Q_{e+1} − Q_s holds for **any** nonnegative initial Q_s. Copying the parent's Q into child buckets, or any other warm start, therefore does not invalidate the children's window bound. It only adds ‖Q_s‖² inside the norm bound. In that narrow sense, "no transfer error" is right. However:
  - (a) A bucket's Q controls only the bucket-aggregated regret Σ_{I∈B} R_I(a). It does **not** control each original infoset's regret during windows when I was pooled. That pre-split regret can grow linearly and is the abstraction error. It is not removed by the split. It is amortized only if pooled time is o(T).
  - (b) Summing per-infoset immediate regrets to bound overall regret (CFR's decomposition) needs perfect recall at the level where regret is summed. For imperfect-recall buckets, see Lanctot et al. 2012 (Q6).
  - (c) Brown & Sandholm (2015) warn that regret transfer can invalidate guarantees when action sets differ. That does not apply to infoset splits that keep the same actions (Q6).
- [D] **Sampling.** In MCCFR the inequality holds for the *sampled* r̃: Σ_W r̃ ≤ Q̃_{e+1}. The true window regret adds the martingale term Σ_W (r − r̃), which is bounded by Azuma or Freedman (Q3). So true window regret ≤ ‖Q̃_{e+1}‖ + O(c√(L log(1/p))).
- [D] **Why RM+ suffers under noise (mechanism; no source found).** With noisy r̃, each Q̃(a) is a reflected random walk. With zero true drift, E[Q̃_t(a)] grows like σ√t even though the clipped true Q stays bounded. With negative drift −μ, Q̃ settles at a stationary level of about σ²/(2μ), from heavy-traffic results for reflected walks. RM+ therefore keeps assigning non-vanishing mass to bad actions and forgets accumulated negative evidence, while RM keeps it as large negative regret. This matches the "aggressive regret matching vs. variance" explanation above. For the requester: **per-original-infoset tracked statistics should be raw (unclipped) cumulative sampled regrets, not RM+-style clipped Q's.** Clipping each noisy per-infoset accumulator biases Σ_I Q̃_I upward by about σ√L per infoset even when there is no heterogeneity.
- [D] **Discounting.** Under DCFR, positive Q is multiplied by d_t = t^α/(t^α+1) < 1 before adding r_t, so Q_{e+1} ≥ (Π d)Q_s + Σ_{t∈W}(Π_{u>t} d_u) r_t. Only a *discounted* window sum is bounded, and the RM+ window argument does not hold as stated. For α=1.5, Π_{u≥s} d_u → 1 as s → ∞, so late windows are nearly undiscounted. Early windows are not. LCFR's linear weights give a bound on Σ t·r_t. Restarts and discounting should not be mixed without re-deriving the window inequality.

## Q6. Prior work using hypothesis tests or confidence bounds on sampled regrets or values to decide pruning, abstraction refinement or stopping

### Takeaway
I found **no** work that applies formal hypothesis tests, Hoeffding or Bernstein confidence intervals, or anytime-valid confidence sequences to **sampled counterfactual regrets inside MCCFR** to decide splits, pruning or stopping. The closest prior art is **CFR+IRA** (Čermák, Lisý, Bošanský; AIJ 2020 / IJCAI 2020). It monitors regrets of a **randomly sampled subset of original infosets**, resamples that subset periodically, splits an abstract infoset when observed regret lags the CFR+ no-regret guarantee, and proves a **probabilistic** bound on average external regret. That is almost exactly the structural idea of the requester's hashed tracked subset. Pruning in practice (RBP, Pluribus) relies on deterministic arguments or fixed thresholds. Statistically rigorous splitting has its closest analogues in stream-mining Hoeffding trees, including the documented pitfalls of applying Hoeffding to nonlinear criteria, and in time-uniform confidence sequences, which have so far been applied only to *evaluation* in poker (AV-AIVAT, 2026).

### Cited Findings
- [V] **CFR+IRA / FPIRA** (Čermák, Lisý, Bošanský): start from a coarse imperfect-recall abstraction and "refine information sets where necessary." CFR+IRA "samples a subset of unabstracted information sets, monitors their immediate regret … for a well-chosen number of iterations before sampling a new subset," and splits an infoset off its abstract infoset when its regret "falls behind the no-regret guarantee." Refinement is triggered when observed convergence is slower than CFR+'s theoretical guarantee. This is a threshold check, not a stated confidence bound. — [IJCAI 2020 extended abstract](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2020/0701.pdf); [arXiv 1803.05392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05392); [AIJ 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370220300126)
- [V] "Since CFR+IRA randomly samples information sets during the abstraction update, we provide a probabilistic bound on the average external regret of the algorithm," and the bound "still holds when also using the heuristic update." The proof sketch argues that iteration counts are large enough that any abstracted infoset preventing low average external regret will be split. It then bounds the probability that the infoset structure allows the desired regret as a function of those counts. The algorithm converges to a Nash equilibrium of the original two-player zero-sum perfect-recall EFG, and in experiments the abstractions are as small as 0.9% of the original infosets. FPIRA is the predecessor (IJCAI 2017). — [arXiv 1803.05392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.05392); [FPIRA IJCAI 2017](https://mlanthology.org/ijcai/2017/cermak2017ijcai-algorithm/)
- [R] CFR+IRA runs full-width CFR+ on the abstraction, so monitored regrets are exact rather than MCCFR-sampled. The only randomness is which infosets are monitored. Verify this in Sec. 4.3 of the arXiv version.
- [V] Brown & Sandholm, "Simultaneous Abstraction and Equilibrium Finding" (IJCAI 2015): **actions** are added at run time. One can use **regret transfer** in the new subgame, or a recovery game. "If S2 has fewer actions than S1, then applying regret transfer would imply that illegal actions were taken, which would invalidate the theoretical guarantees." The rule for when to add an action compares the derivative of summed average regret with and without the added subgame, assuming regrets for actions outside the abstraction grow at rate ∼T. Regret transfer comes from their AAAI 2014 paper. — [IJCAI 2015 PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/15/Papers/075.pdf); [Strategy-based warm starting, AAAI 2016](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/10056/10056-13-13584-1-2-20201228.pdf)
- [V] **Regret-based pruning** (Brown & Sandholm, NeurIPS 2015) skips negative-regret paths for the minimum number of iterations before their regret could turn positive, then makes up the skipped iterations, while keeping CFR's guarantees. This is a deterministic, full-width argument. Follow-ups include best-response pruning and dynamic thresholding. — [NeurIPS 2015](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/hash/c54e7837e0cd0ced286cb5995327d1ab-Abstract.html); [arXiv 1609.03234](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03234); [Dynamic thresholding AAAI 2017](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2017/brown2017aaai-dynamic)
- [V] Li & Huang, EVPA (ICLR 2025), combine expected-value estimates, minimax pruning before CFR, and *dynamic information abstraction* for HUNL. I did not see whether a statistical criterion is used. — [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/8c1b5863a6b0f925617b917bb2f55be0-Abstract-Conference.html)
- [V] Restricted-game expansion as an analogue of split timing: XDO's sample complexity is exponential in restricted-game size because of its exponentially decaying stopping threshold, i.e. it expands too often. PDO (ICML 2023) and AdaDO (2024) fix the expansion frequency and get polynomial complexity. — [arXiv 2411.00954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00954); [PMLR v202 (Tang et al. 2023)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/tang23b.html)
- [V] **Anytime-valid stopping (evaluation, not solving).** AV-AIVAT (Li, Chen, Huang; arXiv, Aug 2026; single preprint) pairs AIVAT with confidence sequences "valid under continuous monitoring." Across 71,439 HUNL hands, raw outcomes needed a median 74× more hands than AIVAT-corrected outcomes to stop under an *asymptotic* confidence sequence. The *exact* empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence requires a justified bound on corrected payoffs, established only for Leduc; its descriptive stopping ratio on HUNL was a median 1.37×. The value model learns only from past games. — [arXiv 2608.06362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06362)
- [V] Confidence sequences (Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, Sekhon; Ann. Statist. 49(2), 2021): time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences. They include an empirical-Bernstein bound with LIL-rate width, generalizations of the SPRT to sub-Gaussian and Bernstein conditions, and matrix martingales. — [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-49/issue-2/Time-uniform-nonparametric-nonasymptotic-confidence-sequences/10.1214/20-AOS1991.full)
- [V] **Hoeffding trees** (Domingos & Hulten, KDD 2000) split a leaf when the gap between the two best attributes' criterion values exceeds the Hoeffding ε at confidence 1−δ, with a tie-breaking threshold. — [KDD 2000 PDF](https://www.cs.rhodes.edu/welshc/COMP465_S15/Papers/kdd00.pdf). **Documented pitfall:** Rutkowski et al. (IEEE TKDE 2013) show "the Hoeffding inequality is not appropriate" for information gain or Gini, which are not means of independent terms, and derive McDiarmid-based bounds instead. Matuszyk et al. (2013) give a "correctedVFDT" that satisfies Hoeffding's prerequisites. Jaworski et al. (2017) repeat the critique. — [Rutkowski TKDE](https://kik.pcz.czest.pl/~rutkowski/papers/TKDE-2011-07-0410.pdf); [Matuszyk et al. 2013](https://kmd.cs.ovgu.de/pub/matuszyk/MatuszykEtAl13.pdf); [Jaworski et al. 2017](https://home.ipipan.waw.pl/j.mielniczuk/Rutk_Jaworski_et_al_2017.pdf)
- [V] Imperfect-recall abstraction theory relevant to bucket cancellation. Lanctot, Gibson, Burch, Zinkevich, Bowling (ICML 2012): CFR minimizes regret in **well-formed** imperfect-recall games. For **skew well-formed** games, average regret is bounded by a term vanishing in T plus Σ_I |P(I)|·δ_I, which is the residual as T → ∞. "There is no guarantee that regret will be minimized by CFR in a skew well-formed game." In imperfect recall generally, the sum of counterfactual regrets no longer upper-bounds external regret. Kroer & Sandholm extend this to bounds for perfect-recall refinements of abstract near-equilibria. — [arXiv 1205.0622](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0622); [ICML 2012 PDF](https://icml.cc/2012/papers/58.pdf); [Kroer & Sandholm, arXiv 1409.3302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3302)

### Inferences
- [D] The requester's design is closest to CFR+IRA, with two differences that need their own analysis. (i) The monitored regrets are **MCCFR-sampled**, not exact, so the split rule needs a noise-calibrated threshold (Q3/Q7). (ii) A **fixed hash** replaces CFR+IRA's repeated re-sampling of the monitored subset. CFR+IRA's probabilistic guarantee relies on re-sampling (see Q7 on Borel–Cantelli). CFR+IRA's "falls behind the no-regret guarantee" criterion is also the natural *null* for the requester. Under H0 (bucket homogeneous enough), a tracked infoset's window regret is O(√L) plus noise. A statistic that grows like Θ(L) indicates heterogeneity.
- [D] **Repeated testing across epochs.** A split test with fixed false-positive rate α, applied at infinitely many epochs with conditionally fresh noise, will by the *second* (conditional) Borel–Cantelli lemma produce **infinitely many false splits a.s.** You need either α_n with Σα_n < ∞ (first Borel–Cantelli gives finitely many false splits a.s.; thresholds grow like √(2L log n)), or a time-uniform confidence sequence per bucket (Howard et al. 2021) with total error budget α. Splits are irreversible, so unbounded false splits mean unbounded memory growth. The algorithm still converges, but toward the unabstracted game.
- [D] The Hoeffding-tree literature shows exactly the trap relevant here. Applying a mean-type concentration inequality to a **nonlinear** functional (information gain there; Σ max(·)^+ of regrets here) is invalid without a bounded-differences (McDiarmid) or martingale-based argument for that functional.
- [D] Pluribus-style fixed-threshold pruning and RBP's deterministic skip argument have no statistical calibration. They should not be cited as precedent for "statistically valid" decisions from sampled regrets.

## Q7. Horvitz–Thompson and importance-sampled estimation of nonlinear functionals (e.g., Σ of max or positive-part of cumulative regrets), plus Paley–Zygmund and conditional Borel–Cantelli

### Takeaway
HT is unbiased only for **linear totals of fixed, exactly observed** unit values with known positive inclusion probabilities. The requester's per-infoset values are **convex functions (max, (·)^+) of noisy sampled regrets**. The plug-in HT estimate of Σ_I max_a R_I(a)^+ is therefore **biased upward**, by Jensen's inequality and the optimizer's curse. Since cancellation equals that sum minus a bucket-level term, splits will be over-triggered unless the threshold accounts for the bias or the bias is removed, for example by cross-fitting on independent halves. Lévy's conditional Borel–Cantelli is the right tool for almost-sure detection. It needs **fresh** randomness in each epoch, which a fixed hash does not provide.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Under **Poisson sampling**, inclusion indicators are independent Bernoulli(π_k). The HT variance is Σ_k (y_k/π_k)²·π_k(1−π_k) = Σ_k (1−π_k)/π_k·y_k², because cross terms vanish. An unbiased variance estimator uses joint inclusion probabilities. — [R `sampling::varHT` docs](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/sampling/html/varHT.html); [jipApprox HTvar](https://rdrr.io/cran/jipApprox/man/HTvar.html)
- [V2/general] A ratio of HT totals is generally biased, with bias of order 1/n for fixed sample sizes, and delta-method variances are standard. This came from the search tool's general knowledge, not a cited text; check Särndal–Swensson–Wretman. — [search context; consistency note arXiv 1412.2887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2887)
- [V] **Optimizer's curse** (Smith & Winkler, *Management Science* 52(3), 2006): choosing the alternative with the largest estimate yields expected disappointment "even if the value estimates are unbiased." — [Duke record](https://fds.duke.edu/db/aas/stat/faculty/bob.winkler/publications/278471). **Maximization bias** (van Hasselt, NeurIPS 2010): using "the maximum action value as an approximation for the maximum expected action value" gives positive bias. A double estimator can *under*estimate. — [NeurIPS 2010 metadata](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2010/file/091d584fced301b442654dd8c23b3fc9-Metadata.json); [estimation bias in double Q-learning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.14419)
- [V] **Lévy's conditional Borel–Cantelli**: for events A_n adapted to a filtration (F_n), {A_n i.o.} = {Σ_n P(A_n | F_{n−1}) = ∞} almost surely. The proof uses the martingale Σ(𝟙_{A_n} − P(A_n|F_{n−1})). — [Wikipedia: Borel–Cantelli lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel%E2%80%93Cantelli_lemma); [arXiv 1103.1426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.1426); [Mathlib docs](https://florisvandoorn.com/BonnAnalysis/docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/BorelCantelli.html)
- [V] The bounded-differences / McDiarmid route for nonlinear split criteria is in the Hoeffding-tree literature above. — [Rutkowski TKDE](https://kik.pcz.czest.pl/~rutkowski/papers/TKDE-2011-07-0410.pdf)

### Inferences
- [D] **Paley–Zygmund** (standard, not re-verified this session): for Z ≥ 0 and θ∈[0,1], P(Z > θ·E Z) ≥ (1−θ)²·(E Z)²/E[Z²]. Apply it to the HT total Z = (1/p)·Σ_{I∈S∩B} y_I under Poisson(p) design with y fixed. Then E Z² = (Σy)² + ((1−p)/p)·Σy², so (E Z)²/E Z² = 1/(1 + ((1−p)/p)·Σy²/(Σy)²). In the worst case, all cancellation sits in one infoset (Σy²/(Σy)² = 1), the ratio is about p, and the per-epoch detection probability is ≥ (1−θ)²·p. That is the probability the single bad infoset is tracked, which makes sense.
- [D] **Borel–Cantelli needs fresh randomness.** To conclude that a heterogeneous bucket is detected i.o. a.s., you need P(detect at epoch n | F_{n−1}) ≥ c_n with Σ c_n = ∞. If the hash seed is drawn once (so it is in F_0) and the offending original infosets are untracked, then P(detect | F_{n−1}) = 0 for every n on that event. The argument then fails on a positive-probability event, e.g. probability (1−p)^{#bad infosets}. **Remedy:** re-salt the hash at each epoch, as CFR+IRA re-samples its monitored subset, or make the argument conditional on the seed and accept detection only "with probability ≥ 1−(1−p)^{n_bad}". Also, the conditional lower bound must include MCCFR noise in y_I, not only design randomness. Paley–Zygmund then needs a bound on E[Z² | F_{n−1}] covering both sources.
- [D] **HT unbiasedness also needs y independent of S.** Once splits are triggered by tracked infosets, every later regret, tracked or not, depends on the past S. Design-based unbiasedness survives only **conditionally**: draw S_n independently of F_{start of epoch n} and measure y over epoch n. A fixed S across epochs breaks this after the first split.
- [D] **The nonlinear plug-in is biased upward (main technical concern).** Let the tracked statistic be y_I = max_a R̃_I^W(a)^+, with window W of length L and R̃ = R + N, where N is a martingale with per-iteration conditional variance about σ_I². Jensen gives E[y_I] ≥ max_a (R_I^W(a))^+. For near-tied actions the excess is up to about σ_I√L·E[max of |A| standard normals] ≈ σ_I√(2L ln|A|). Summed over a bucket of n_B infosets, the HT statistic under H0 (homogeneous bucket, true per-infoset window regret O(√L) via the bucket's RM+ bound) is shifted by Θ(n_B·σ̄·√(L log|A|)). Under H1 (persistent heterogeneity with gap g>0), the signal is Θ(n_B·g·L). The test is consistent if the threshold grows faster than √(L log|A|) (plus a multiplicity term √log n over epochs) and slower than L. **A fixed threshold on a √L-scaled or raw statistic will either over-split (false positives) or under-split.**
- [D] The bucket-level term is not symmetric. max_a(Σ_I R̃_I(a))^+ averages noise over n_B infosets before the max, so its bias is about σ̄√(n_B L log|A|), much smaller than the Σ of per-infoset biases (n_B·σ̄√(L log|A|)). The **difference** (cancellation estimate) is therefore biased upward by about n_B·σ̄√(L log|A|)·(1 − 1/√n_B). If the bucket term uses the RM+-clipped Q_B (≥ raw R_B), the estimate shifts downward. If the per-infoset terms are clipped accumulators, the upward bias is larger (Q5).
- [D] **Debiasing options.**
  - (1) *Cross-fitting / double estimator*: split each window's ES iterations into two independent halves (e.g. odd/even iterations, or two independent traversals per iteration), select â_I = argmax on half 1, and evaluate R̃_I(â_I) on half 2. This removes selection bias at some underestimation cost, as in van Hasselt.
  - (2) *Quadratic heterogeneity statistic*: use, e.g., T_B = Σ_I ‖R_I − w_I R_B‖² estimated **unbiasedly** by the cross-product of independent halves, since E⟨X¹, X²⟩ = ‖μ‖² when X¹, X² are independent with mean μ. Smooth or polynomial functionals admit unbiased U-statistic estimators. max/(·)^+ generally do not ([R] e.g. |θ| for a normal mean has no unbiased estimator).
  - (3) *Calibrated null*: estimate the H0 bias from the empirical per-infoset variances (Freedman or empirical-Bernstein) and subtract it, or use a confidence sequence.
- [D] **Heavy tails.** ES with perfect recall gives bounded increments (Q1), so second moments are fine. If OS, AS or probing is used anywhere, y_I inherits 1/q weights and Paley–Zygmund's E[Z²] can blow up. The detection lower bound then becomes vacuous, though not false.
- [D] **"Almost-sure detection" is not error control.** It says nothing about finite-T false-split rates. Pair it with summable α_n or a time-uniform confidence sequence (Q6) to get both a.s. eventual detection of persistent heterogeneity *and* finitely many false splits a.s. Also, if the heterogeneity gap g_n of a bucket shrinks along the run (e.g. as opponent strategies converge, so that Σ_n P(detect | F_{n−1}) < ∞), a.s. detection can fail. The argument needs g_n bounded away from 0 or Σ c_n = ∞ explicitly.

- I did not source-verify Paley–Zygmund or the textbook form of Lévy's lemma (Durrett, *Probability: Theory and Examples*).

## Q8 (synthesis). Which pieces of the requester's scheme are standard, which are known to be problematic, and what prior statistical-testing ideas apply

### Takeaway
**Standard and sound:** ES-MCCFR with unbiased per-infoset regrets; high-probability no-regret via Azuma or Freedman (Farina 2020) for any minimizer including RM+; restarting per epoch with geometric windows; warm-starting split children with nonnegative Q; Lévy's conditional Borel–Cantelli as a proof device. **Known problematic:** RM+ (CFR+) under sampling, which is empirically worse than RM, LCFR or DCFR without variance reduction or mini-batching, and is unstable even without noise; Chebyshev-type (√(1/p)) bounds for multi-test calibration; mean-type concentration applied to nonlinear functionals. **Specific to the requester (derived):** the window guarantee is only O(√T) unless Q is reset. The HT cancellation statistic is upward-biased. A fixed hash breaks the Borel–Cantelli and HT conditions after the first split. Repeated fixed-α testing gives infinitely many false splits. **Closest prior art:** CFR+IRA (random monitored subset, regret-vs-guarantee split rule, probabilistic regret bound).

### Cited Findings
- [V] ES high-probability bound (1+√(2/p))·Δ·M_i·√|A_i|/√T — [Lanctot et al. 2009 appendix](https://mlanctot.info/files/papers/nips09mccfr_appendix.pdf). Improved to √log(1/p) via Azuma — [Farina et al. 2020](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/farina20a/farina20a.pdf).
